Current sky: `{sun: {azimuth_deg, elevation_deg, intensity, color, source, driven_by_clock}, clouds: {enabled, coverage, density, bottom_km, thickness_km, size_km, detail, wind_speed, wind_dir_deg, shadows}, sky: "procedural" | "hdri", hdri: {path, loaded, width, height, sun_found, error?, intensity, rotation_deg, sun}}`. While the scene has a weather (`weather_get`), its cloud cover and wind drive the clouds.

Sun angles: `azimuth_deg = atan2(d.z, d.x)` and `elevation_deg = asin(-d.y)` of the light's travel direction d. Elevation 90 is noon overhead, 5..10 is golden hour, and below 0 is night. This azimuth is a world-space angle (from +X toward +Z), not a compass bearing: for the compass bearing of the real Sun use `time_get`. `sun` is the light the scene is lit with, and `sun.source` says what positions it: `"hdri"` (the loaded panorama's own sun, while `sky` is `hdri` and `hdri.sun` is on; it outranks the clock), `"clock"` (a world clock from `time_set`; change the time instead of the angles) or `"manual"` (set it with `environment_set`). `sun.driven_by_clock` is true only for `"clock"`. Under `"hdri"` with `hdri.sun_found` false (an overcast or interior panorama) the sun is off: its intensity is 0 and its angles mean nothing.

`sky` is `hdri` only after a panorama was requested (`environment_set`). It renders only while `hdri.loaded` is true; until then the procedural sky stands in. `sun_found` tells whether the panorama has a sun that can drive the sun light.
