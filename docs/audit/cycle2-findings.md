| D1 | H | **Process hangs on exit while a GPU terrain tile is pending.** New `Terrain` components default to `erosion_model = grid` + `gpu_generate = true`, so `generate_tile_async` returns a `std::promise`-backed future from `GpuTileSource::generate`. That promise is only fulfilled from `TerrainPass::add_to_graph` (frame loop) or `~GpuTileSource`. Teardown calls `terrain_job.wait()` after the loop has stopped and while `terrain_pass` (which owns the source) is still alive, so `wait()` never returns. Scenario: `QUAT_TERRAIN=1 QUAT_MAX_FRAMES=3 QUAT_SCREENSHOT=x.ppm` (513² tile, 200 steps ≈ 4 frames + readback), or closing the window or the editor while "generating..." is shown, means the process never exits (headless CI runs time out). The same pending future also never progresses while `terrain_present` is false (the Terrain entity is deleted mid-generation), and `!terrain_job.valid()` then gates every later regenerate on it. | `src/main.cpp:1923` (wait), `:1446-1449` (GPU job), `src/renderer/terrain_gpu_tiles.cpp:24,32-38` | Before the teardown wait, drop GPU-backed jobs: either `terrain_job = {}` when the job came from a `TileGenerator`, or destroy or `shutdown()` the `GpuTileSource` (which sets every pending promise) before `terrain_job.wait()`. Also clear or abandon the job when the Terrain entity goes away. | verified |
| D2 | M | **`debug.time_scale` and `debug.step` desynchronise physics from the other sim systems.** `sim_dt = real_dt * scale` goes unchanged to `nav_agent_system`, `bt_system`, `scene.tick` (animation) and `character_update`. `PhysicsWorld::update`, however, runs at most 8 × 1/120 s = 0.0667 s per call and `fmod`s the rest away (B7 fix). At 60 fps with scale 10 (allowed max), `sim_dt` = 0.167 s: rigid bodies advance 0.4× as far as agents, animations and characters. A `debug.step` with `dt` in (0.0667, 0.1] (accepted, `max_step_dt = 0.1`) silently loses up to a third of each step for bodies. This breaks the header's promise that each step advances "exactly step_dt" and makes stepped traces wrong. | `include/engine/core/sim_clock.hpp:29-31`, `src/main.cpp:1119-1241`, `src/physics/physics_world.cpp:227-242` | Clamp `max_step_dt` to `max_steps_per_update * fixed_timestep`, and either cap the time scale so that `real_dt*scale` stays under that budget or let the sim-clock path call `PhysicsWorld::update` in ≤0.0667 s slices (the hitch cap is for real-time backlog, not for requested sim time). | verified |
| D3 | M | **Remote terrain parameters allow effectively unbounded CPU erosion work and a ~3.5 GB transient allocation.** `terrain.resolution` accepts up to 8193 and the new `erosion_steps` up to 100 000. Any tile the GPU path refuses (res > 2049, or `gpu:false`) runs `erode_grid` on the CPU. `GridSim` plus the `eroded` copy allocate about 52 B/texel, which is 3.5 GB at 8193². Under `-fno-exceptions` a failed allocation aborts. If the allocation succeeds, 100 k steps over 67 M texels take hours. Meanwhile the main thread blocks in `~TerrainStreamer` (a `std::async` future) on the next regenerate or streaming toggle, every later single-tile regenerate is gated on `terrain_job.valid()`, and exit blocks in `terrain_job.wait()`. One `component.set terrain {"resolution":8193,"erosion_steps":100000}` from a remote client wedges the engine. (Droplets were bounded to roughly 50 M droplets; the grid path is new.) | `src/scene/component_json.cpp:732,739`, `src/terrain/generator.cpp:349-361`, `src/terrain/grid_erosion.cpp:37-41` | Validate the combined cost (`resolution² × steps` budget) in the codec, clamp `erosion_steps` to the inspector's 5000, refuse grid erosion on the CPU above the GPU's `max_resolution`, and give the CPU job a cancel flag checked per iteration. | verified |
